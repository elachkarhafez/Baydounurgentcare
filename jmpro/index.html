    What the chalk is for

    When we find damage we circle it, count it and take the picture. These marks are from real JM Pro inspections.

    Hail bruise

    Hail knocks the granules off and leaves a soft spot. It looks small now and leaks later.

    Circled for the adjuster

    Every hit gets marked before the photo, so your insurance company sees what we saw.

    Counted slope by slope

    We count the hits on each side of the roof and write it right on the shingles. That count goes in your report.

    Dented vents

    Hail dents metal too. Vents, turbines and gutters get checked along with the shingles.

    Torn shingles

    Wind can rip pieces right off. We outline the spots so you can see exactly where they are.

    Then you get a straight answer

    No scare tactics. If your roof still has years left, we'll tell you that.

    It's in good shape

    You still get the photos, so you know where it stands. Nothing to sign.

    It needs a repair

    Plenty of roofers say replace the whole thing. We find the source of the leak, photograph it and fix that.

    It's storm damage

    We put together the impact photos and paperwork your adjuster needs, and we'll meet them at the house. Kentucky gives you 12 months to file, so don't sit on it.
